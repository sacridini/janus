Seasons · sub-annual index

Phenology, step by step.

Every field greens up and dries down on its own calendar. The Phenology tool finds the growing seasons in a vegetation index series, fits a curve to each, and reads when it starts, peaks and ends: on the chart for one pixel, and as maps for the whole image.

01The idea

The tool is Zeit's phenology, written from the papers listed below (Whittaker: Eilers 2003; HANTS: Roerink et al. 2000; the curves of Beck 2006, Elmore 2012, Gu 2009, Klosterman 2014, Zhang 2003, the asymmetric Gaussian of Jönsson & Eklundh and the double logistic of Fisher et al. 2006), not from the code of phenofit (Kong et al. 2022), which stays a useful reference to compare outputs with. It smooths the series to find its seasons, from one trough to the next; fits a curve to the observations of each season (a double logistic, by default Elmore's); and reads from the curve:

SOS
Start of season: the day the curve rises past the threshold (by default, half of the season's amplitude).
POS
Peak of season: the day of the curve's maximum.
EOS
End of season: the day it falls back past the threshold.
LOS
Length of season: EOS minus SOS, in days.

It needs a sub-annual vegetation index (NDVI, EVI; 8 or 16 days, or monthly) with at least six observations a year. Dates on the maps are days of the year.

02The example

The same MODIS EVI series as the BFAST guide: every 16 days from 2001 to 2025, 250 m, over 170 × 170 km of farmland in western France. The pin is on a field.

How these screenshots were made

The data is the author's own, not downloaded: MODIS EVI every 16 days, 2001–2025, of western France, one GeoTIFF with a band per date, used as it is (the BFAST guide's). These commands remake the screenshots after the interface changes.

The screenshots, saved as PNGs in the folder shots (STACK.tif is the EVI file):

janus --shots phenology STACK.tif shots

The tour drives the guide's steps in a window of 1680 × 1050 and saves the window and its panels as PNGs in shots; the full-window ones are then converted to JPEG.

03On the chart

Tools → Temporal dynamics → Phenology. With the chart fit on, the seasons' curves are drawn over the series, with vertical lines at each start, peak and end, and the Statistics table lists the seasons.

The peaks move: 5 May in 2012, 5 June in 2014, 1 July in 2015. That is the crop rotation: a winter cereal peaks in spring, sunflower or maize in summer. Hover the map to fit any other pixel.

In Janus

  1. Open the MODIS EVI stack of the BFAST guide with File → Open.
  2. Drop a pin on pixel 71, 319, a field with a strong season every year.
  3. Open Tools → Temporal dynamics → Phenology and tick Fit the cursor, pins and ROI series. The parameters are the defaults.
  4. The chart draws the smoothed curve and the seasons; the Statistics table gives their start, peak and end.

In Python

"""Janus 1.17.0 (2026-11-07): pin 1 of EVI_Series_ID_EU_150-X026-Y027_2001_2025.tif, reproduced outside Janus with Zeit.
Needs: pip install "zeit-cdts>=3.2"
"""
import numpy as np
import zeit

# Pin 1: column 71, row 319 of the files, its centre at x 3481625, y 2607125 (EPSG:3035)
series = zeit.load_raster(
    "EVI_Series_ID_EU_150-X026-Y027_2001_2025.tif", masked=True, pixel=(71, 319)
)
series = series.squeeze("band", drop=True)  # the bands are dates of one named band
values = series.values.ravel()
# the gaps filled linearly (by date index), with weight 0: they pull neither the smoothing nor the curves
ok = np.isfinite(values)
idx = np.arange(len(values))
filled = np.interp(idx, idx[ok], values[ok])
series = series.copy(data=filled.reshape(series.shape))
weights = ok.astype(float).reshape(series.shape)
result = zeit.phenology(series, curve='elmore', weights=weights, annual=False, max_seasons=52)

print(result)

The code Janus’s Copy Python button gives for this pixel and these parameters; it runs with Zeit: the header of the script says which Zeit it needs (pip install zeit-cdts, version 3.2 or later; 3.2.1 for CCDC, CODED and S-CCD). Copy and Download give the same script, with the file’s name as it is here (put the script beside your data).

04The parameters

ParameterDefaultWhat it does
CurveelmoreThe function fitted to each season: Beck, Elmore, Gu, Zhang (double logistics), asymmetric Gaussian, Klos, or the double logistic of Fisher et al. (2006).
Season start/endtrs5How SOS and EOS are read: a threshold of the amplitude (50%, 20%, 60%), the derivative, or Zhang's or Gu's methods.
SmoothingwhittakerUsed to find the seasons (the curves are fitted to the observations): Whittaker, HANTS or none.
Whittaker lambda10Larger is smoother: fewer, broader seasons.
HANTS frequencies3Harmonics a year, with HANTS.
Min pixel amplitude0.1Pixels whose series varies less (water, towns, bare rock) are skipped. In the index's units: 1000 for NDVI × 10000.
Min season amplitude0Seasons that rise less above their troughs are dropped.
Min season length0Seasons shorter than this, in days, are dropped.
Season mappedmedianHow a pixel's seasons become one map: the median of all, the latest, or the one peaking in Year.

For a field with a double crop, or grassland cut twice, lower lambda (or raise the HANTS frequencies) until the chart splits the year into two seasons; for a noisy series, raise it.

05Running it

Phenology fits a curve to every season of every pixel: 0.46 million pixels and 25 years here, about 6 minutes on every core (the window estimated 8). It runs in the background; the map and the chart stay usable.

06The maps

With Season mapped on the median, each map is the typical year of each pixel:

MapMeaning
SOS, POS, EOSStart, peak and end of the season, day of the year.
Length of seasonEOS − SOS, in days.
Peak valueThe index at the peak.
AmplitudeThe rise of the season above its troughs.
Fit R²How well the curves fit the observations.
Seasons detectedHow many seasons the pixel has.
