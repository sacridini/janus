Raster time series analysis · Windows · macOS · Linux · What’s new in 1.21.1

See the past of every pixel.

Janus is a desktop app for satellite image time series: hover any pixel to see its history, find where, when and how much the land changed with LandTrendr, CCDC, BFAST and 33 more methods, and measure how accurate your maps are. No code and no cloud account: it runs on your computer, on your files.

For people who map land change with Landsat, Sentinel, MODIS or any stack of rasters.

Three steps

From a stack of rasters to a map you can defend.

01

Explore

Open a folder, a multiband file or a wildcard: every date is a layer. Hover any pixel and its series is on the chart; click to pin it, pin a second one to compare, or draw a region for its mean with a p10–p90 band.

Seeing a series The workspace

02

Detect

Fit a method on the chart for one pixel and change its parameters until the fit reads the landscape. Then run it on the whole image: its year of detection, magnitude and duration come back as layers over the map.

The methods The change track LandTrendr guide

03

Prove

Stratify the map, draw a sample, interpret it blind with chips of every date and a high-resolution image of every year (Esri Wayback, in the style of Matthias Baumann’s Validation Viewer), and get the accuracy and the error-adjusted areas with confidence intervals (Olofsson et al. 2014). In the guide's example the map counts 5,674 ha of change; the sample estimates 5,262 ± 882 ha.

How it works

Every pixel has a past.

Each date is one layer of the cube. Janus keeps a reduced copy of all of them in GPU memory, computes the per-pixel statistics in a shader and, while the mouse rests, reads the full-resolution series of that pixel from disk in the background.

Features

Made for looking at time.

Open

  • Any stack of rasters A folder, a multiband file, a list of files or a wildcard; dates from file names or band descriptions.
  • Series from STAC Earth Search, Planetary Computer, the Brazil Data Cube or any STAC API, read in place, downloaded or composited, clouds masked.
  • Several bands per date Any band of a Landsat or Sentinel stack, or a normalized difference of two; a quality band hides the clouds.
  • Radar Sentinel-1 RTC in dB, one orbit pass at a time, with Quegan's or Lee's speckle filter.
  • Full resolution on zoom Tiles of the visible area are read in the background; the overview is cached on disk, so reopening takes a fraction of a second.
  • Categorical series Land cover stacks are detected from colour tables or names; the table reports the last change.

See

  • Hover, pin, compare An approximate series as you hover, the exact one when the mouse rests; Shift+drag for a region's mean and p10–p90 band.
  • Statistics on the GPU 13 map modes: mean, trend, amplitude, anomaly and more; changing the date, colormap or stretch is instant.
  • Side by side Several series as layers placed by their georeferencing, in map panels that pan and zoom together; drag one to a second monitor.
  • Swipe A divider across the map to compare a layer with another, or with itself at another date or mode.
  • Transect Distance against time in one image: fronts and regrowth appear as shapes.
  • A series seen whole Similarity map, cycles by wavelet, every band over time and a UMAP plane linked to the map.
  • Everything a keystroke away Ctrl+K finds any menu, tool, index or layer; a status bar; blend modes, groups, a minimap and a lens.

Analyse

  • Band math over time The 275 Awesome Spectral Indices, or an expression of the bands, reduced over time, by year or month, with lags, anomalies and rules.
  • Change detection built in 36 methods fitted live on the cursor and pins, or run on the image, with the results added as map layers.
  • Difference and largest drop The change from a reference date or from the previous one, or when the largest drop happened and how deep.
  • Accuracy and area Stratify, sample, interpret blind, and get accuracy and error-adjusted areas with confidence intervals.
  • Classify by the whole series TempCNN, L-TAE, U-TAE or Presto on your samples, on the GPU and without Python; a class map, its probability, its uncertainty and the area of applicability.
  • Segments of alike series SNIC: each segment's mean series as a layer, their polygons as a vector layer.
  • Fields as parcels WRI's Trazo and Fields of The World's networks draw each field's boundary on the GPU; fine-tune them on fields you draw.

Out & reproduce

  • Out to your tools PNG at up to 4× the screen resolution, GeoTIFF of the values or the view, CSV of the series.
  • Series in and out Build a series from loose files, put an irregular one at a fixed step, save a multiband COG, a NetCDF or a Zarr ready for xarray.
  • Areas, polygons and zones Reproject, polygonize, the hectares of every class at every date, rasterize, zonal statistics through time.
  • Projects, undo, provenance A project keeps layers, styles, dates, views, pins and ROI; Ctrl+Z undoes; every result records how it was made, to run again or copy as Python.

Install · latest release

Nothing else to install.

Each package carries GDAL and the PROJ and GDAL data; Zeit is part of the program. No Python or conda needed.

Windows

Run the installer. It installs per user, without an administrator prompt, and can add jn to PATH, an Open in Janus entry to the right-click menu of folders and .tif files, and a desktop shortcut.

Download .exe

janus-<version>-setup.exe

macOS Apple Silicon

Open the disk image and drag Janus into Applications. The app is not signed with an Apple Developer ID: the first time, open it with right click → Open. To call it from a terminal, link it once:

ln -s /Applications/Janus.app/Contents/MacOS/janus ~/.local/bin/jn
Download .dmg

janus-<version>-macos-arm64.dmg · macOS 15 or later

Linux x86_64

Extract the archive anywhere and run jn from that folder. It is portable: only glibc, the graphics drivers and X11 or Wayland come from the system. janus.desktop is included for a menu entry.

Download .tar.xz

janus-<version>-linux-x86_64.tar.xz · tested on Ubuntu 24.04

Then, from a terminal

jn D:\data\ndvi_annual\
jn ~/data/landsat_stack.tif
jn "S2_*_NDVI.tif" --band 1

After installing, jn opens the window and gives the prompt back right away.

Prefer to build it yourself? Building from source needs Rust and GDAL.

Command line

Open it, set it up, script it.

jn opens the window and gives the prompt back at once. Beyond what to open, its options set the window up the way you want it: a tool ready on the chart or already run, pins, the calculator, a screenshot.

--band N
band used when each file is one date
--budget MB
memory for the cube overview, this run only (default: Settings, 1024)
--threads N
processing threads, this run only (default: Settings; HDD = 1)
--help
every option, in the terminal
Terminal
# a folder (1 file = 1 date), a multiband file (1 band = 1 date),
# a wildcard
jn ndvi\
jn landsat_stack.tif
jn "S2_*_NDVI.tif" --band 1

# a Zeit tool fitted on the chart, and a pin at pixel X,Y
jn ndvi\ --zeit landtrendr --pin 1272,1414

# a parameter of the tool (TOOL.ID=JSON), and the run on the raster;
# with --roi X0,Y0,X1,Y1 the run is on that window
jn ndvi\ --zeit landtrendr --zeit-param landtrendr.max_segments=8 --zeit-run

# the raster calculator open with a text (statements apart by ;), and run
jn s2 --calc "ndvi = (N - R) / (N + R); median(ndvi)" --calc-run

# the window as a PNG once the series is ready, then exit
jn ndvi\ --zeit landtrendr --pin 1272,1414 --screenshot fit.png

For AI assistants

jn mcp

A server of the Model Context Protocol on the standard input and output. An assistant such as Claude Desktop or Claude Code works with your series through Janus's own engines, with no window and no Python: jn mcp [--threads N]. Every result is a file (GeoTIFFs, VRTs, GeoPackages, CSVs, models) and its path comes back in the answer; open_in_janus shows it in the window you have open.

  • Lookdescribe a series, read its values at places and over areas, list the spectral indices, read vector files.
  • Calculatecheck and run raster calculator expressions over named layers.
  • Detectlist, describe and run Zeit's tools (BFAST, CCDC, LandTrendr, trends, phenology...) on a series or on maps.
  • Prepareregularize, speckle filter, harmonize sensors, reproject, rasterize, polygonize.
  • Measurezonal statistics and the area of each class or change year.
  • Classifytrain a Random Forest, TempCNN, L-TAE, Presto or U-TAE on samples, and classify a series with the model.
  • Find datasearch STAC catalogs and build a series from the scenes found.

27 tools in all; the list and the setup for each client. In Claude Code: claude mcp add janus -- jn mcp --threads 8

Questions

Before you open a cube.

Is Janus a GIS?

No. It is made for time: open a series, move through it, inspect any pixel, detect change with the Zeit tools and validate the maps they make. For editing vectors or laying out maps, keep using your GIS: Janus hands it GeoTIFFs, PNGs, CSVs and GeoPackages.

Is it free?

Yes. Janus is free and open source under the GPL-3.0-or-later, as is Zeit, the library of its methods. The source, the releases and the issues are on GitHub.

Which files can it open?

Anything GDAL reads, as a folder of rasters (one date per file), a multiband file (one date per band), a list of files or a wildcard pattern; also cloud-optimized GeoTIFFs by URL, NetCDF and Zarr, and the scenes of a STAC catalog (File → Search STAC catalog...). Cloud-optimized GeoTIFFs open fastest the first time.

Do I need Python for the Zeit tools?

No. Zeit's methods are compiled into Janus: nothing runs in Python, and nothing starts until you use a tool.

Can an AI assistant drive it?

Yes, through jn mcp, a Model Context Protocol server: an assistant such as Claude Desktop or Claude Code can describe a series, read its values, run the calculator and the Zeit tools, train and apply classifiers and search STAC catalogs, with the results as files. What it exposes.

Will a spinning hard disk be slow?

Janus detects spinning disks and reads them with a single sequential reader, which was measured to be four times faster than two. The first open is bound by the disk; after that the cube comes from the overview cache. The cache is written date by date, so if you close Janus halfway, the next open reads only the dates still missing.

Why the name?

Janus is the Roman god of doorways and of time, shown with two faces: one looks at the past and one at what comes next. That is what you do with a pixel's series. Up to version 0.16 the program was called tsv.

Built in Rust with egui, wgpu and GDAL. Direct3D 12 on Windows, Vulkan on Linux, Metal on macOS.
